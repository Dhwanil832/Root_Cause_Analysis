# Human Involvement

## Purpose

Explore how people interacted with the work and the hazard without equating human involvement with human error.

## Activate this tag when

- A worker, contractor, supervisor, witness, or other person performed, directed, observed, or was exposed to the work.
- Capability, qualification, authorization, experience, fatigue, workload, physical positioning, PPE, ergonomics, or work practice may matter.
- The event depends on what a person knew, perceived, decided, communicated, or was able to do.

## Do not use this tag to

- Assign blame because a person was present.
- Treat an action as causal before examining the conditions that shaped it.

## First-pass branching questions

1. Who was involved or exposed, and what role did each person have?
2. What was each person trying to accomplish immediately before the event?
3. What actions or non-actions are factually established, and what remains assumed?
4. What information, cues, instructions, or warnings were available at the time?
5. What qualifications, training, authorization, or task familiarity were relevant?
6. Were fatigue, overtime, workload, physical capability, ergonomics, or time pressure potentially relevant?
7. What PPE or protective clothing was expected, available, and observed?
8. How did the task setup, equipment, environment, or organization shape the person's available choices?

## Branches and handoffs

- Missing, unclear, or impractical work instruction: activate Procedure / planning involvement.
- Handover, signals, coordination, supervision, or escalation issue: activate Communication / supervision involvement.
- Equipment design or condition shaped the action: activate Equipment or tool involvement.
- Access, visibility, congestion, weather, height, or atmosphere shaped the action: activate Work-environment involvement.
- Qualification or staffing affected non-routine work: activate Maintenance, outage, or non-routine work.

## If this tag was processed before

Reuse established roles, actions, qualifications, schedule information, PPE facts, and task objectives. Ask only questions needed to resolve:

- conflicting accounts of an action or observation;
- a newly relevant person or role;
- an unverified qualification, authorization, fatigue, or PPE condition;
- the reason an action made sense in the conditions present;
- a newly discovered organizational or environmental influence.

Do not ask people to retell the entire incident when the sequence is already documented.

## Expected result

Record established human interactions, contextual influences, relevant capability or exposure gaps, disputed facts, and handoffs. Avoid labels such as careless, complacent, or operator error unless the underlying evidence and conditions are documented.
