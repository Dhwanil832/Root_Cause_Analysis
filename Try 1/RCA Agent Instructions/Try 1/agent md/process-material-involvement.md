# Process or Material Involvement

## Purpose

Explore whether process conditions, material properties, containment, handling, reactions, temperature, or process variability contributed to the event.

## Activate this tag when

- Molten metal, slag, steel, coils, strip, plate, scrap, chemicals, corrosives, dust, gases, fumes, steam, hot surfaces, or process fluids are involved.
- Welding, cutting, burning, heating, mixing, casting, charging, pouring, or other process activity may matter.
- Material condition, contamination, moisture, temperature, shape, weight, stability, or incompatibility may matter.

## First-pass branching questions

1. What process and material were involved, and what condition were they expected to be in?
2. What were the actual temperature, pressure, composition, moisture, quantity, shape, or physical state?
3. What containment, separation, cooling, ventilation, or handling controls applied?
4. What changed in the process or material before the event?
5. Was the material compatible with the equipment, container, environment, and task?
6. What signs of leakage, buildup, contamination, reaction, overheating, instability, or loss of containment existed?
7. What process monitoring, sampling, alarms, inspections, or quality checks were available?
8. What evidence could distinguish a material problem, process excursion, equipment failure, or handling issue?

## Branches and handoffs

- Container, valve, pipe, furnace, tool, or equipment condition: activate Equipment or tool involvement.
- Heat, pressure, gravity, or other energy release: activate Stored, released, or gravity energy.
- Fume, gas, dust, temperature, or exposure conditions: activate Work-environment involvement.
- Manual or operator interaction: activate Human involvement.
- Process standard, limit, recipe, work sequence, or monitoring plan: activate Procedure / planning involvement.
- Maintenance or changed process configuration: activate Maintenance, outage, or non-routine work.

## If this tag was processed before

Reuse the process state, material identity, measured conditions, containment, monitoring, and timeline. Ask only about unverified properties, newly discovered contamination or variation, conflicting process data, or evidence that distinguishes remaining process and material hypotheses.

## Expected result

Record the expected and actual process/material states, deviations, containment and handling conditions, exposure or release paths, evidence gaps, and handoffs.
