# Fixed scenario — R3-16515

Operator/evaluator only. Frozen design, 2026-09-22; no investigator output was used to select outcomes for this package.

## Anchor and extension

The historical workbook reports incident 16515: the R3 exit carrier beam and link fell into the pit during an outage, with the keeper still installed, rolls removed, balance raised, hydraulics isolated/bled, and no crane lift. Its root-factor field says **Still investigating**. The separately supplied partner RCA PDF proposes wedging, relative yoke movement, off-center clearance and hydraulic bleed-through; its chart contains undefined statuses, an A/B hydraulic naming discrepancy and pending checks. It is a supplemental causal hypothesis source, not raw proof.

The exercise makes the following world fixed through explicitly synthetic records. With backup rolls absent, the carrier beam had reached housing interference surfaces. Contact held the beam while the balance/yoke moved downward. Relative movement and lateral displacement let the link clear the yoke's retaining geometry without breaking the keeper. Gravity then carried the beam and link into the pit. The small liner fell during the same episode; its exact initiating role remains unresolved.

For this reconstruction only, the traced circuit has a loaded cylinder volume connected through isolation valve IV-R3 to a header that had been depressurized. A closed-handle valve permits reverse leakage in a later workshop observation. The local header gauge does not sense the trapped load-side volume. This is a possible supported drift path, not proof that zero pressure existed everywhere or that valve leakage alone was sufficient to make the beam fall. The physical geometry and timing are independently necessary to the fall explanation.

The scene survey finds no independent restraint on the exposed assembly; incident-time footage covers only the visible yoke region, so absence of every possible hidden restraint is not established. The issued task record has no task-specific raised-load restraint entry, while a general practice calls for an identified load-control method. Blank paperwork is evidence of missing documentation, not itself proof of physical absence. Why the task documentation omitted the configuration remains unknown.

## Live alternatives and terminal boundaries

- Fracture or missing keeper: initial inspection weighs against either, without proving all retention functions adequate.
- External crane impact: reported absence of lifts weighs against it; it does not rule out every possible disturbance.
- Beam and yoke descend together: later separately tracked reference points contradict this for the observed interval.
- Complete hydraulic immobilization: header indication and closed-handle position alone do not establish this.
- A liner strike initiates the release: neither confirmed nor fully excluded by the available sampling.
- Cylinder internal leakage or other hydraulic paths: no complete cylinder diagnostic is supplied. IV-R3 reverse leakage is a supported path, not the exclusive proved path.

Exact clearances, forces, leakage rate, component dimensions, pre-event keeper torque, valve defect origin, individual motives, legal compliance and effectiveness of proposed changes remain unknown. Do not score a numeric stability calculation or a definitive valve-only root cause. Engineering review is pending; status is `technical_review_needed`.

## Design changes from prior R3 exercises

No blank form is converted into confirmed missing hardware. No keeper fracture is invented. Hydraulic header pressure is distinguished from cylinder pressure. The supplied RCA's interpretations are not mislabeled as spreadsheet findings. The three-release package is a new protocol, not a replay of earlier four-version outputs.
