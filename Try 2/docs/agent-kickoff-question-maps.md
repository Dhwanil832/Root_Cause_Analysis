# Agent kickoff question maps

These tables are design exemplars for the specialist prompts. They are not mandatory questionnaires. Each question demonstrates a useful opening move and the investigation directions it may expose. An agent should select, combine, reword, skip, or expand these examples according to the evidence, and it may generate new questions when a material direction is not represented.

A question should be asked only when its answer could confirm, distinguish, redirect, or screen out a material causal direction.

## Human involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| Who was involved or exposed, and what role did each person have? | Worker, contractor, supervisor, operator, witness, bystander; assigned responsibility, authority, exposure location |
| What was each person trying to accomplish immediately before the event? | Task objective, competing priorities, production pressure, troubleshooting goal, informal work, mismatch between assigned and actual work |
| What actions or non-actions are factually established, and what remains assumed? | Verified sequence, decision points, perception, response, omitted action, unreliable reconstruction, premature human-error conclusion |
| What information, cues, instructions, or warnings were available at the time? | Briefing, alarm, label, signal, visibility, language, comprehension, missing or conflicting information; may activate Communication / supervision |
| What qualifications, training, authorization, or task familiarity were relevant? | Competency, certification, authorization, contractor qualification, refresher training, unfamiliar or non-routine task |
| Were fatigue, overtime, workload, physical capability, ergonomics, or time pressure potentially relevant? | Shift length, staffing, workload, fatigue, distraction, reach, force, posture, physical limitation, schedule pressure |
| What PPE or protective clothing was expected, available, and observed? | PPE selection, availability, condition, fit, compatibility, use, enforcement, hazard assessment |
| How did the task setup, equipment, environment, or organization shape the person’s available choices? | Equipment design, access, visibility, procedure practicality, supervision, staffing, incentives, line of fire; handoff to the relevant specialist |

## Electrical incident

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What electrical asset, circuit, or system could be involved? | Equipment/tool, wiring, panel, battery, controls, temporary power, upstream or downstream circuit |
| What was the confirmed energy state immediately before and during the event? | Energized work, unexpected energization, stored energy, induced voltage, battery power, unknown or unverified state |
| What work, interaction, or change was occurring around the electrical system? | Operating, maintenance, testing, troubleshooting, installation, cleaning, switching, physical contact |
| What evidence suggests an electrical event or abnormal condition? | Shock, arc flash, heat, smoke, tripped device, damaged insulation, exposed conductor, abnormal equipment behavior |
| What was different from the normal equipment or work condition? | Temporary wiring, outage configuration, bypass, recent repair, damaged component, changed setup, wet or contaminated condition |
| What protection or control was expected to prevent exposure, and what was its observed state? | Isolation / LOTO, breaker, fuse, relay, interlock, grounding, guarding, PPE; may activate equipment or procedure paths |
| Who or what could have been exposed, and by what possible path? | Human involvement, direct or indirect contact, conductive path, nearby personnel, contractor exposure, equipment damage |
| Were surrounding conditions relevant to the electrical exposure or failure? | Work-environment involvement: water, dampness, confined access, poor visibility, conductive surfaces, congestion |

## Equipment or tool involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What equipment, tool, component, or interface was involved? | Machine, hand tool, power tool, guard, fixture, attachment, structural component, connection or interface boundary |
| What was it expected to do, and what did it actually do? | Functional deviation, failure to move, unintended movement, drift, release, jam, loss of control, abnormal response |
| What was its physical and operating condition before and after the event? | Wear, fracture, deformation, corrosion, contamination, overheating, misalignment, damage pattern, latent defect |
| Was it the correct equipment or tool for the task and conditions? | Selection, suitability, compatibility, capacity, rating, substitute tool, improper application; may activate Procedure / planning |
| Had it been inspected, maintained, repaired, modified, or reported defective? | Missed inspection, maintenance quality, recurring defect, modification, temporary repair, deferred work, defect reporting |
| What setup, alignment, guarding, support, clearance, or attachment condition existed? | Installation error, removed guard, temporary support, inadequate restraint, interference, loose attachment, outage configuration |
| What operating limits, loads, tolerances, or failure modes may be relevant? | Overload, fatigue, tolerance stack-up, design margin, pressure, speed, temperature, foreseeable failure mode |
| What physical evidence could distinguish failure, misuse, unsuitable design, or external influence? | Fracture surface, witness marks, measurements, logs, testing, photographs, debris, sequence evidence, expert examination |

## Mobile equipment / vehicle involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What mobile equipment or vehicle was involved, and what task was it performing? | Forklift, loader, truck, trailer, cart, tractor; travel, loading, unloading, towing, parking, positioning |
| What was its movement, position, speed, load, and direction immediately before the event? | Backing, turning, stopping, acceleration, rollaway, unstable load, blind-side travel, collision sequence |
| What route, travel surface, clearance, traffic pattern, and exclusion zone existed? | Poor surface, slope, congestion, restricted clearance, traffic-plan weakness, pedestrian separation, route change |
| What could the operator and nearby people see or hear? | Blind spots, mirrors, cameras, alarms, lighting, noise, obstruction, line of sight, pedestrian awareness |
| What was the equipment’s condition, including brakes, steering, tires, alarms, lights, restraints, and attachments? | Mechanical failure, missed pre-use inspection, maintenance issue, disabled warning, tire or brake condition, attachment defect |
| Was the load stable, secured, within capacity, and compatible with the equipment? | Overcapacity, shifted load, poor center of gravity, incorrect attachment, inadequate securement, visibility obstruction |
| Who controlled or directed the movement, and how was coordination handled? | Operator qualification, spotter, signal, radio, movement authority, conflicting direction; activates Communication / supervision |
| What changed from the normal route, task, equipment, load, or surrounding activity? | Temporary route, substitute vehicle, unusual load, outage congestion, simultaneous work, changed traffic control |

## Crane, lifting, or suspended-load involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What lifting equipment, rigging, attachment, and load were involved? | Crane, hoist, sling, hook, magnet, chainfall, lifting beam, attachment point, load identity |
| What stage of the lift or suspended condition existed at the time? | Rigging, initial take-up, hoisting, travel, rotation, landing, release, parked suspension, residual suspended state |
| What were the load’s weight, center of gravity, geometry, stability, and attachment points? | Unknown weight, shifting center of gravity, unstable geometry, inadequate pick points, unbalanced or changing load |
| Were the crane, hoist, rigging, and lifting devices suitable and within rated capacity? | Capacity, load chart, rigging angle, side loading, component compatibility, below-the-hook rating, overload protection |
| What inspections, condition checks, and lift planning applied? | Pre-use inspection, rigging condition, critical-lift plan, engineering review, missed defect, unsuitable or absent plan |
| Who operated, rigged, signaled, directed, or worked near the lift? | Operator, rigger, signaler, lift director, qualification, role clarity, positioning; Human involvement |
| What line-of-fire, exclusion-zone, clearance, swing, or travel conditions existed? | People beneath or near load, pinch point, swing radius, overhead obstruction, wind, congestion, inadequate barricade |
| What evidence explains any unintended movement, release, contact, shift, or fall? | Rigging failure, attachment release, control input, overload, contact, structural failure, gravity path, event sequence |

| What movement or rail task was occurring, and what was the intended route? | Switching, coupling, uncoupling, spotting, loading, securement, authorized route, unintended movement |
| What were the positions and securement states of the rolling stock before the event? | Hand brakes, chocks, air brakes, gradient, uncoupled cars, rollaway, incorrect consist position |
| What was the condition of the track, switches, derails, brakes, couplers, and warning systems? | Track defect, switch alignment, derail position, brake failure, coupler defect, signal or warning malfunction |
| Who controlled, authorized, directed, or observed the movement? | Engineer, conductor, ground person, dispatcher, contractor, movement authority, qualification, role clarity |
| What signals, radio calls, confirmations, or switching instructions were used? | Missed or misunderstood signal, radio failure, readback, confirmation, conflicting instruction; Communication / supervision |
| What clearance, fouling, crossing, pedestrian, vehicle, or loading conditions existed? | Fouled track, close clearance, crossing conflict, pedestrian exposure, vehicle interaction, loading interference |
| What changed from the planned consist, route, load, switching sequence, or operating condition? | Route change, added car, unusual load, altered sequence, temporary track condition, maintenance or outage activity |

## Stored, released, or gravity energy

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What form or forms of energy were present? | Gravity, hydraulic, pneumatic, pressure, spring, tension, thermal, steam, compressed air, mechanical or rotational energy |
| Where was the energy stored, supported, restrained, or generated? | Raised component, pressure vessel, accumulator, cylinder, line, spring, counterweight, suspended or tensioned object |
| What prevented the energy from being released during normal conditions? | Support, keeper, pin, brake, valve, block, restraint, interlock, structural member, pressure-control device |
| What changed immediately before the movement or release? | Removal, loss of pressure, control action, vibration, component failure, contact, temperature change, altered configuration |
| What object, fluid, component, or person was within the potential energy path? | Line of fire, crush zone, fall zone, pressure discharge, moving component, nearby structure, exposed worker |
| What blocking, support, restraint, pressure control, or secondary protection existed? | Primary and secondary blocks, cribbing, mechanical stop, check valve, relief device, redundant support, exclusion zone |
| Was the amount or direction of energy understood and accounted for in the work? | Energy assessment, load or pressure calculation, hidden energy, unexpected direction, incomplete hazard recognition |
| What physical evidence could identify the release point, sequence, and failure of containment or support? | Damage pattern, fracture, displaced parts, gauge or control records, witness marks, fluid path, photographs, measurements |

## Isolation / LOTO involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What equipment or system was being isolated, and for what work? | Isolation scope, affected equipment, maintenance boundary, task dependency, wrong or incomplete equipment identification |
| What hazardous-energy sources and boundaries were identified? | Electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, gravity, thermal, chemical, mechanical, stored or secondary energy; boundary completeness |
| What isolation points, locks, tags, blocks, blanks, disconnects, or valves were used? | Correct isolation point, missing device, ineffective valve, wrong disconnect, absent block, lock or tag application |
| How was zero energy or the intended safe state verified? | Tryout, meter test, pressure check, visual verification, drain or bleed, test-before-touch, assumed rather than verified state |
| What stored, residual, induced, gravity, or secondary energy remained possible? | Accumulator pressure, trapped fluid, elevated load, induced voltage, spring force, thermal energy, backfeed |
| Who established, controlled, changed, or relied on the isolation? | Authorized employee, affected employee, contractor, supervisor, system owner, authority, handoff and reliance |
| Were group, contractor, shift-change, testing, temporary-energization, or restoration conditions involved? | Group lockbox, transfer, turnover, exception, test energization, lock removal, startup and restoration sequence |
| What evidence confirms the isolation state at each important point in the event sequence? | LOTO sheet, lockbox log, switching record, photographs, meter readings, valve position, witness statement, timestamped state |

## Maintenance, outage, or non-routine work

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What work was planned, and what was actually being performed? | Planned versus actual scope, emergent work, troubleshooting, informal task, scope drift, conflicting work orders |
| What equipment, process, or area was placed in a non-normal condition? | Shutdown, opened system, bypass, removed guard, disabled control, isolated area, changed process or access |
| What had been removed, disabled, bypassed, opened, supported, or temporarily configured? | Missing component, temporary support, defeated interlock, open boundary, abnormal load path, incomplete reassembly |
| What work occurred on previous shifts or by other crews and contractors? | Shift handoff, incomplete work, changed state, contractor interface, undocumented action, ownership gap |
| What scope changes, discoveries, delays, or conflicting activities occurred? | Change control, new hazard, schedule pressure, simultaneous operations, work conflict, contingency planning |
| What condition was the system expected to be left in between work stages? | Interim safe state, temporary configuration, responsibility, tagging, preservation, unattended equipment condition |
| What inspections, hold points, testing, and return-to-service steps applied? | Missed verification, premature release, incomplete testing, signoff, commissioning, restoration or startup control |
| What evidence documents the configuration and work sequence? | Work order, outage schedule, shift log, photographs, permits, configuration drawing, contractor record, control-system history |

## Process or material involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What process and material were involved, and what condition were they expected to be in? | Normal process state, material specification, molten metal, chemical, gas, coil, scrap, fluid, expected containment |
| What were the actual temperature, pressure, composition, moisture, quantity, shape, or physical state? | Overtemperature, overpressure, contamination, moisture reaction, unstable shape, excess quantity, unexpected phase |
| What containment, separation, cooling, ventilation, or handling controls applied? | Vessel, piping, barrier, cooling, exhaust, segregation, transfer method, loss of containment or control |
| What changed in the process or material before the event? | Process excursion, feed change, startup or shutdown, blockage, buildup, contamination, changed recipe or material source |
| Was the material compatible with the equipment, container, environment, and task? | Chemical incompatibility, unsuitable container, thermal effect, moisture contact, equipment rating, handling mismatch |
| What signs of leakage, buildup, contamination, reaction, overheating, instability, or loss of containment existed? | Leak path, residue, pressure rise, smoke, discoloration, unusual odor, deformation, unstable load or reaction |
| What process monitoring, sampling, alarms, inspections, or quality checks were available? | Alarm response, missing instrument, trend data, sampling gap, inspection frequency, out-of-limit condition |
| What evidence could distinguish a material problem, process excursion, equipment failure, or handling issue? | Samples, lab results, historian data, damage pattern, equipment inspection, batch record, witness account, CCTV |

## Work-environment involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| Where did the event occur, and what physical features of the area were relevant? | Area layout, pit, platform, confined space, aisle, elevation, equipment proximity, restricted or unusual access |
| Were height, access, openings, ladders, scaffolds, stairs, or changing elevations involved? | Fall exposure, scaffold or ladder condition, open hole, access method, edge protection, elevation transition |
| What were the walking and working surfaces, housekeeping, drainage, and congestion conditions? | Slip, trip, uneven surface, debris, water, poor drainage, blocked route, material congestion, unstable footing |
| What lighting, visibility, noise, weather, temperature, or wind conditions existed? | Poor visibility, glare, noise masking, heat or cold stress, rain, ice, wind effect, communication difficulty |
| Was dust, fume, gas, oxygen level, ventilation, or another atmospheric exposure possible? | Toxic or combustible atmosphere, oxygen deficiency, welding fume, dust, vapor, asbestos, inadequate ventilation |
| What clearances, barriers, exclusion zones, escape paths, and lines of sight existed? | Line of fire, inadequate barricade, restricted egress, blind area, pinch point, pedestrian or equipment separation |
| Was the area in its normal condition, or had work, deterioration, or temporary arrangements changed it? | Outage scaffold, temporary barrier, removed floor section, deterioration, construction, maintenance staging, changed access |
| What photographs, measurements, monitoring results, inspections, or witness observations establish the conditions? | Scene documentation, atmospheric readings, lighting level, dimensions, weather record, inspection, corroborated observations |

## Procedure / planning involvement

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What work was intended, and what governing procedure, plan, permit, or assessment applied? | Work scope, procedure, JSHA, permit, risk assessment, checklist, undocumented or misclassified work |
| Did the document match the actual task, equipment, hazards, people, and conditions? | Generic or outdated procedure, wrong asset, omitted hazard, contractor mismatch, field conditions not represented |
| What critical steps, limits, hold points, prerequisites, and contingencies were defined? | Missing step, unclear limit, absent verification, weak prerequisite, no contingency, uncontrolled decision point |
| What resources, staffing, time, tools, and information were planned and available? | Understaffing, unavailable tool, insufficient time, missing drawing, skill mix, production pressure, resource conflict |
| What changes or discoveries occurred, and how were they assessed and authorized? | Scope change, emergent condition, management of change, stop-work, approval, informal workaround |
| Was the sequence workable, understandable, and consistent with how the job was actually performed? | Impractical sequence, ambiguous instruction, work-as-imagined versus work-as-done, deviation driven by field reality |
| What known hazards and previous lessons were incorporated? | Repeat event, missed learning, outdated risk assessment, known defect, absent historical control, weak organizational learning |
| What evidence shows how the plan was briefed, used, revised, or departed from? | Pre-job briefing, signoff, field copy, revision history, permit record, observed practice, deviation or approval trail |

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| Who needed to communicate or coordinate, and for what decision or action? | Crew interfaces, contractor coordination, operator-maintainer boundary, movement authority, isolation or work ownership |
| What information was sent, received, understood, confirmed, or missing? | Incomplete message, misunderstanding, no readback, missing warning, conflicting information, language or comprehension gap |
| What communication method was used, and was it suitable for the conditions? | Radio, hand signal, face-to-face instruction, written handoff, noise, visibility, channel failure, unsuitable medium |
| Who had authority and responsibility for the work, movement, isolation, or area? | Unclear ownership, overlapping authority, absent decision-maker, contractor role, supervisor accountability, stop-work authority |
| What shift, contractor, department, or crew handoffs occurred? | Turnover quality, changed equipment state, incomplete work, undocumented condition, responsibility transfer |
| What supervision, verification, or field presence was expected and available? | Span of control, absent supervisor, verification point, remote direction, inadequate field oversight, staffing |
| Were concerns, changes, conflicts, or abnormal conditions raised and resolved? | Escalation, stop-work, unresolved concern, normalized deviation, production conflict, feedback failure |
| What records or independent evidence establish the communication sequence? | Shift log, radio recording, text or email, permit, briefing record, witness accounts, timestamped control data |

|---|---|
| What animal, wildlife, or insect was involved, and how was it identified? | Species or agent, bite or sting risk, contamination, behavior, mistaken identification, required response |
| Where and when was it encountered, and was the presence expected or previously reported? | Habitat, seasonal pattern, recurring sighting, prior warning, inspection history, location-specific exposure |
| What contact, bite, sting, startle, distraction, contamination, or movement occurred? | Direct injury, allergic response, startled movement, loss of balance, distraction, contaminated area or equipment |
| Were nests, food sources, openings, seasonal conditions, or habitat factors present? | Entry point, nesting site, attractant, housekeeping, vegetation, weather, facility deterioration, pest-management need |
| What immediate medical, first-aid, containment, or area-control response occurred? | Treatment delay, allergy protocol, emergency response, exclusion zone, containment, decontamination, reporting |
| What pest-management, inspection, warning, or reporting controls existed? | Preventive inspection, pest contractor, missing warning, reporting pathway, recurring unresolved condition |
| Did the encounter create a secondary exposure such as a fall, vehicle movement, dropped object, or equipment contact? | Stored energy, mobile equipment, work at height, dropped object, line of fire, unintended machine interaction |
| What evidence establishes the encounter and its role in the event? | Photograph, specimen, medical finding, witness account, nest or entry point, pest record, scene evidence |

## Other / novel condition

| Initial question | Potential direction it opens |
|---|---|
| What condition or subject is not represented by the existing tags? | Precise definition of the potentially novel direction; prevents Other from becoming a general uncertainty bucket |
| What facts show that it is materially connected to the event? | Evidence of relevance, temporal connection, mechanism, exposure, consequence; screens out interesting but immaterial details |
| Which existing tag is closest, and why is it insufficient? | Taxonomy boundary, possible reassignment, overlap, hidden distinction, need for a genuinely separate investigative path |
| What distinct investigation questions does this condition require? | Unique mechanism, evidence source, subject-matter expertise, control family, causal branch not covered elsewhere |
| Is it genuinely novel, or is only the wording or specific object unusual? | Synonym resolution, site terminology, unusual equipment still covered by Equipment/tool, avoid unnecessary new tags |
| Has the same theme appeared in other incidents under different language? | Recurrence, historical pattern, taxonomy normalization, broader category, trend analysis |
| What evidence could confirm or screen out this direction quickly? | Discriminating test, record, expert review, inspection, measurement, interview, rapid reassignment |
| Should the condition remain a case-specific note or be proposed for taxonomy review? | One-off condition, recurring gap, new permanent tag candidate, governance and taxonomy-change decision |
