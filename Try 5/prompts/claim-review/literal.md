# Review one original claim

Your job is to decide what the supplied documents establish about target.statement. Keep the complete original statement intact. Do not split it into nouns, time phrases or a meaning tree, expand unexplained acronyms, or replace it with an easier claim. Treat ordinary terms in their ordinary contextual sense. Use the evidence to resolve terminology; do not invent doubts about clear words, dates or units.

## Scope calibration examples — not incident evidence

These contrasts demonstrate the task, not facts to copy into this investigation.

Evidence: an event register lists Room 12 and leaves the interpreter-name field empty. It contains no attendance observation.
- Claim: "The register lists Room 12, but no interpreter attended." Result: partially-established. The room entry is established; interpreter attendance is not. A sentence beginning with a document does not turn a later claim about reality into a claim about that document. Do NOT silently append "in the register" to the attendance clause.
- Claim: "The register lists Room 12 and the interpreter-name field is empty." Result: entailed, record-observation. This says only what the register contains; no attendance claim is made.

Evidence: two observers inspected the entire room at noon, with full visibility, and reported it empty.
- Claim: "No interpreter was in that room at noon." Result: entailed, actual-state. Do not reject a scoped absence that a complete contemporaneous observation establishes.

Evidence: one supplied page of a multi-page invoice lists the room charge. A billing guide requires invoices to list room charges. No complete invoice is supplied.
- Claim: "The invoice records a room charge and contains no meal charge." Result: partially-established. The room charge is shown; neither a selected page nor a rule about required entries proves the absence of other entries across the invoice.
- Claim: "The supplied page contains a room-charge entry." Result: entailed. Its scope is the supplied page, not the complete invoice.

Apply the same distinction even when the surrounding incident makes the stronger conclusion plausible. Do not use the lack of contradictory passages as positive evidence for an absence claim.

## Evidence comparison

First select the original lines needed and briefly state what they establish. Then compare that evidence with the exact assertion, preserving its subject, event, location, time, negation and alternatives. A line must support the meaning you use, not merely contain similar words. Include every source needed for a multi-document conclusion.

Choose one relation:
- entailed: the entire original assertion is established in its stated scope.
- partially-established: a genuinely compound statement has one complete assertion established and another left open. State the missing premise. Do not award partial support merely because a disputed measurement's port name or timestamp is known.
- contradicted: evidence disproves the assertion in the same scope. Missing information is not disproof.
- not-established: the asserted fact is unresolved, including when relevant accounts conflict.

Report the claim type without changing its meaning. Record-observation means a claim about what a record says or what a field contains. Actual-state/event means what existed or happened, even if the sentence also mentions paperwork. Mixed means the statement combines these scopes. Requirements concern rule text; hypotheses propose possibilities. These labels are routing metadata, not evidence.

A form's empty field establishes an empty field, not absence of the object/action it could record. A complete physical inspection can establish scoped absence. A document saying what a work package should contain does not establish its actual complete contents. An excerpt cannot establish that the entire archive contains no relevant entry. An observation after an event does not automatically establish the earlier state. Preserve an allowed OR alternative when judging compliance.

Use direct, clear evidence: do not ask for the definition of a routine word when the record explicitly supplies the requested fact. List only necessary information still missing AFTER reading the evidence. Missing premises block support; they do not erase a cited disproof. Optional questions about causes do not undo an established observation. Never infer a failure solely from a missing record.

Separately report material source conflicts. Cite BOTH opposing accounts, the shared subject/location/time and the evidence needed to resolve them. Different times, equipment, operating states or units are not automatically contradictions. A single record disproving a claim is not a source conflict.
- impact=claim-truth: incompatible accounts leave the truth of this exact assertion unresolved. Do not select one account as the physical truth without evidence resolving the dispute.
- impact=underlying-event-only: the exact assertion is about what a record says; that content can be established while another source disputes the described reality.

An established record observation is not itself a demonstrated incident mechanism. Ask a follow-up only if it resolves a concrete investigation gap. Return a concise evidence comparison and decision, not a private reasoning transcript. Finish when this claim is assessed; do not reconstruct the whole RCA.
