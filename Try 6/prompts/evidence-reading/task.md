# Purpose
Build an evidence notebook, NOT a causal conclusion. Your job succeeds when another investigator can use the source's facts, requirements, definitions, and limitations later. A reference-only document can contain essential knowledge even when it reports no accident, fault, or violation. Do NOT return no findings merely because no cause is described.

Read the supplied ORIGINAL passages closely. Preserve measurements with their instrument/location/time/unit, requirements with every alternative, and reported observations with their attribution. Tables must be read using their headers and surrounding definitions. Separate what the source says from what it establishes.

Extract useful atomic findings and assign relevant specialist tags. Requirements are not proof of compliance; testimony is not independently verified. Use kind=hypothesis only for an explicit interpretation, not to disguise speculation as extraction. Attach all necessary S references. Set replaces=null unless an explicitly supplied old finding is genuinely being corrected; ordinary rewording is not a correction.

Ask only baseline/context questions that the passage makes necessary and does not answer. Never fabricate the missing part of a source or assume a component's usual function applies to this plant.

This task reads ONE selected original passage, not the whole investigation. Preserve scope notes and definitions that change the meaning of a record (for example scheduled versus executed). Classify those as context; do not convert them into observations of equipment failure. Do not extract every heading, document ID, or filename as an independent claim. Group inseparable qualifiers with their fact. When a source attributes uncertainty, keep that attribution in the statement itself. There is no fixed question quota: zero questions is appropriate when this passage introduces no necessary unanswered question.

Before finishing, account for each substantive paragraph and table row: operational observations and timeline changes; physical layout relationships; measurement meaning; applicable requirements and permitted alternatives; supplied document revisions; recorded qualifications; scope limits. Retain every distinct useful item in the notebook without repeating identical findings. This is coverage checking, not a quota and not permission to invent missing information. Do not bury distinct timeline events or document revisions inside another finding's qualifiers.

Examples: “A running light is not a flow measurement” is a useful context finding. “The instruction must be revised OR an approved temporary instruction supplied” is a requirement finding retaining both alternatives; it does not prove either happened. “The completion box is blank” is a record observation, not proof the work was not completed. Document coordinates belong in references, not in the physical location field. Only tag a domain actually implicated by the statement; pressure does not automatically imply an electrical incident. Empty tags are allowed when no specialist domain is indicated.
