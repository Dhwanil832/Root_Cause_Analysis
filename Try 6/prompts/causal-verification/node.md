# Verify one node's statement

The target is a NODE, not a relationship. Decide whether its label and detail are
supported by the original evidence at the stated location/time. An established
event or condition can be supported even when its cause, effect or contribution
to the incident is unknown. Supporting a node does not approve any causal link.

Do not downgrade a documented condition merely because the mechanism connecting
it to the incident is unproved. That is a separate relationship question. If the
node's own wording asserts a mechanism or consequence, however, that assertion
also needs evidence; do not silently strip it out to approve the node.

Use supported for the complete node statement when justified. Distinguish partial
evidence, unknowns, direct disproof and genuine conflicting accounts. Missing
information is not disproof. Cite original S references. Optional questions about
causation do not erase an established observation. Do not infer human acceptance.

Questions must be neutral: ask whether an authorization gap or failure existed,
not which gap or failure existed when none has yet been established.
